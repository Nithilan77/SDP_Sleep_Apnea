What is this project?

Sleep apnea means repeated pauses in breathing during sleep. It is common and mostly undiagnosed, because the standard test is an overnight stay in a sleep lab. We are testing whether one small chest sensor could flag people who should get that test.

1 · One chest sensorST SensorTile.box PRO: records the heart signal (ECG) and chest movement.
→
2 · Two signalsHeartbeat timing changes during apnea. Chest motion shows whether breathing stops.
→
3 · ModelsTrained on public sleep-study datasets with expert labels.
→
4 · A flag“This stretch looks like breathing pauses.” A screening hint, not a diagnosis.

What we are trying to show

Heart and breathing signals together tell more than the heart alone, and the sensor can be made to work on our own hardware.

The catch

Our volunteers are healthy, so we cannot train on our own apnea. The learning has to come from public clinical datasets, and then be adapted to our sensor.

What we tried

  • Classical rules on heart-rate swings (the textbook “cyclic variation of heart rate” pattern).
  • Neural networks (CNN, TCN, GRU, LSTM) on the beat-to-beat interval series.
  • Feature-based models (gradient boosting, logistic regression) on heart-rate-variability measures.

2Why we moved to MESA

What PhysioNet could not give us

  • Only a single ECG lead: no breathing signal.
  • No oxygen (SpO2) and no event types (obstructive / central / hypopnea).
  • Our sensor records chest motion too, so we needed data with a breathing-effort signal.

What MESA gives us

  • Full overnight sleep studies with ECG + chest/abdomen effort belts + SpO2.
  • Expert-scored events with type labels.
  • 220 usable subjects ({{'{:,}'.format(156826)}} half-minute epochs) after our quality checks.

3What we did with MESA

We built a two-stream network (CANet): one stream reads ECG, one reads breathing effort, and they exchange information through cross-attention. We then compared it with using each signal alone, a simpler way of joining them, and versions that also use SpO2.

4Our own data

Overnight recordings

10usable healthy recordings, 0.8 to 7.5 hours each (about 9 volunteers)

ECG plus accelerometer from our SensorTile. No apnea labels, so they act as negative controls: a good model should flag little.

Breath-hold sessions

7voluntary ~30-second breath-holds, one volunteer

A safe stand-in for “breathing stops”, timed with a stopwatch. A voluntary hold is not an apnea event and is never labelled as one.

The problem we found: a domain gap

The MESA-trained network was designed for chest belts. Fed our accelerometer signal from healthy sleepers, it raised an alarm on most of the night:

5Transfer learning to our sensor

Idea: keep what the MESA network learned, and only teach a small final layer what our sensor's breath-holds look like.

How

  1. Freeze the MESA-trained network (nothing inside it changes).
  2. Build two kinds of input from our recordings: deep features (256 numbers from the frozen network) or 8 simple breathing-motion measures (size, spikiness, breathing rate, regularity…).
  3. Train a small, heavily regularised linear classifier on the 7 labelled holds.
  4. Test leave-one-event-out: train on 6 holds, test on the 7th, repeat. Windows overlapping the held-out hold are removed.
